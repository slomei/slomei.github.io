Our Mission

Mental health is a community responsibility.

The Homer Project was created in response to the real — and often unseen — challenges individuals face with stress, trauma, and mental health struggles. Drawing from experience in public safety and crisis response environments, we deliver mental health awareness presentations to schools, workplaces, and community organizations.

Our sessions focus on recognizing stress, building emotional resilience, reducing stigma, and encouraging individuals to seek support when they need it. The goal is simple: strengthen support systems and build a culture where mental health is openly acknowledged.

Meet Homer

A presence that calms the room.

The Homer Project is named in honor of Homer, a certified facility dog trained through Puppies Behind Bars. Homer represents calm, trust, and connection in high-stress environments.

Sometimes the most powerful intervention isn't a word. It's a presence that tells you you're not alone.

He demonstrates — every time he walks into a school, a station, or a community space — the powerful impact that presence and support can have during difficult moments. He is the heart of this work.

Response Model

Five steps when someone may be struggling.

A simple framework for engaging with anyone who might need support. Use it at work, at school, at home.

01
Notice
Watch for changes in behavior, mood, or performance. You don't need to diagnose — just pay attention.
02
Check In
Start a conversation with simple, respectful communication. "I noticed you've seemed off lately — how are you doing?"
03
Listen
Without judgment, without interruption, without rushing to fix. The listening is the help.
04
Connect
Link them to appropriate support resources when needed — professionals, hotlines, trusted people in their lives.
05
Follow Up
Check in again. Don't ignore warning signs. Consistency is what turns a moment into a lifeline.
Know The Signs

Six indicators worth paying attention to.

No single sign means crisis, and these are never a substitute for professional judgment. But awareness starts here.

Withdrawal or isolation
Noticeable mood or behavior changes
Irritability or emotional sensitivity
Fatigue or lack of motivation
Decline in performance or engagement
Appearing overwhelmed or disconnected
